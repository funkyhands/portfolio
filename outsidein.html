Outside In — Urban Living

Architecture · 20.103 Architecture Core Studio 3

Overview collage

Outside In proposes a dense urban housing block that dissolves the boundary between interior living and the exterior landscape. Cascading communal green terraces weave through the massing, bringing sky, greenery, and shared outdoor life directly into the residential experience.

Site

The site is located at Lorong 8 Toa Payoh, at the edge of three distinct urban conditions — low-rise residential to the west, a bus depot, and light industrial sheds. Wedge-shaped and bounded by the Central Expressway, this boundary condition informed the stepped massing: the building acts as a transition between the low-scale neighbourhood and the larger infrastructure beyond.

Aerial context — Toa Payoh, Singapore

Aggregation & Massing

The design began from an aggregation study — exploring how a single bar unit could be stacked, terraced, and folded to respond to the triangular site geometry while maximising green terrace exposure. The chosen scheme steps the massing in section, generating a continuous terraced landscape from street level to the building's peak.

Aggregation studies — from unit scatter to final massing scheme

Final massing in urban context

Programme

The building layers four programmes across the massing: the existing ground-level structures, residential units on the upper floors, communal facilities at terrace junctions, and continuous green spaces woven through every level. Green space is not concentrated at the rooftop — it runs through the full section.

Programme distribution (left) · Green spaces (right)

Plans

The typical floor plan wraps around two large open green courtyards. A public walking path loops the full perimeter of each terrace level, with shared spaces — communal kitchens, laundry, seating — positioned at the points where the path passes between units.

Public walking path highlighted in red

Four unit types range from 130 m² family units down to 36 m² micro-units, creating a genuinely mixed-income housing model on a single floor plate.

Unit type 1 — 130 m² (left) · Unit type 2 — 90 m² (right)

Unit type 3 — 60 m² (left) · Unit type 4 — 36 m² (right)

Section

The section is the primary design tool. Residential floors are offset against green terrace voids, creating a vertical landscape of light and vegetation that penetrates the full depth of the building. Human circulation follows these terraces — every journey through the block becomes an outdoor experience.
